# TerAustralis Incognita — Current Status Brief

For: Sam Maher, GovTechAu

Date: 19 June 2026

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CURRENT STATUS

TerAustralis Incognita is a Sydney-based coordination layer (ABN 70 741 068 059) focused on regulatory and stakeholder navigation for large-scale space infrastructure in Australia.

Published proof point: SpaceNews op-ed (May 2026) on leveraging AUKUS and southern geography for dual-use space infrastructure. Currently mapping the regulatory and policy pathway to enable Starship recovery, refurbishment, and critical minerals integration in the Pilbara region.

Early-stage: we have the strategic framing, site-specific feasibility scopes, and Indigenous partnership principles drafted. We do not have government buy-in, formal agreements, or regulatory clearance. We are pre-decision.

SPECIFIC CAPABILITY WE'RE SEEKING TO ESTABLISH

A formal regulatory and stakeholder coordination pathway that:

Maps the actual (not theoretical) federal and state decision-makers and approval bodies needed for Starship recovery operations in Dampier/Karratha.

Identifies the specific regulatory instruments (ASIC, Defence/ITAR, environmental, state planning, port authority) and their current status relative to commercial space recovery infrastructure.

Establishes a governance structure for Indigenous partnership that meets FPIC standards and is defensible to both Traditional Owners and federal/state regulators.

Creates a bridge between government, industry (SpaceX, Gilmour Space, minerals sector), and defence/AUKUS stakeholders so the technical teams can focus on building without navigating regulatory friction.

We are not trying to be the prime mover or operator. We are trying to be the coordination layer that removes specific, named obstacles.

DECISIONS / APPROVALS WE THINK ARE NEEDED

(In sequence, not parallel)

MONTH 1–2 (July–August):

☐ Formal feasibility assessment co-sponsored by Australian Space Agency and state government (WA or QLD depending on site). This legitimizes the project and signals intent.

☐ Indigenous consultation framework endorsed by Traditional Owner representatives before any site engineering begins. (Ngarluma/Yindjibarndi for Dampier; Juru for Bowen.)

MONTH 3–6 (September–November):

☐ AUKUS Pillar 2 alignment memo from Defence/government acknowledging space infrastructure as dual-use strategic asset. This is the political cover for moving forward.

☐ State-level agreement in principle from WA Government (or QLD) for port/site access, environmental assessment scope, and Indigenous partnership framework.

MONTH 6–12 (December 2026 onwards):

☐ Regulatory clarification from ASIC/Space Agency on commercial recovery permits and liability frameworks for ocean splashdown recovery.

☐ ITAR/technology safeguards agreement confirmation that US–Australia partnership (Starship recovery) is within scope of existing frameworks.

WHERE WE'RE CURRENTLY BLOCKED

(And where we think you can help)

BLOCK 1: Government entry point

We have op-ed credibility, site-specific analysis, and early traction with industry: we've engaged with Gilmour Space (Brisbane), Hancock Prospecting (Pilbara), and the METS Space Cluster. We do not have a named contact inside federal government (Space Agency, Defence, DFAT) who has read our material and wants to move it forward. Gina Rinehart has publicly called for Australia-SpaceX alignment; we need government to recognize this as the opportunity it is and move at the same pace as private sector momentum in the Pilbara.

YOUR INPUT: Who is the right federal stakeholder (Space Agency, Defence, DFAT, or state equivalent) to brief on the AUKUS/dual-use framing? Who understands that Gina's position on SpaceX investment + Pilbara assets = strategic advantage? And what would unlock a conversation with them?

BLOCK 2: Regulatory map

We have fragments: Space (Launches and Returns) Act 2018, COPUOS guidelines, emerging STM norms. We don't have a current, authoritative map of which Australian regulator owns which decision for a Starship recovery operation. ITAR requirements. Port authority approvals. Environmental assessment triggers.

YOUR INPUT: Is there an existing government-maintained regulatory decision tree for commercial space recovery in Australia? If not, who would maintain it? ASA? Attorney-General's?

BLOCK 3: Indigenous partnership legitimacy

We have principles (FPIC, self-determination, benefit-sharing, governance representation). We don't have sitting relationships with Ngarluma/Yindjibarndi or Juru representatives, and we don't have a government-endorsed process for how to do this without it becoming performative or tokenizing.

YOUR INPUT: What is the current best practice for government-sanctioned Indigenous partnership in major infrastructure projects? Who do we approach, and in what sequence?

BLOCK 4: AUKUS positioning

We have the logic: southern hemisphere redundancy, allied supply chain, strategic resilience. We don't have language or a sponsor inside the AUKUS conversation that would make a space infrastructure project feel like a natural fit, rather than something we're forcing into a defence framework.

YOUR INPUT: How is AUKUS Pillar 2 currently being scoped? Is there existing conversation about space dual-use capability? If so, who's driving it?

WHAT WE'D LIKE FROM A 20-MINUTE CONVERSATION

(In priority order)

1. Government entry point: a name, a portfolio, or a process we should be aware of.

2. Confirmation that the regulatory map question is real, or direction to where it's already been solved.

3. One or two names of people inside government (DFAT, Defence, Space Agency, or state economic development) who have shown appetite for space infrastructure partnerships and might care about AUKUS positioning.

4. Any red flags we haven't seen yet—regulatory, political, or strategic.
