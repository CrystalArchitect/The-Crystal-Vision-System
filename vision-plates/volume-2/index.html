CrystalVision · Vision-layer image archive · Volume II

Sixty-six plates,
and five that hold up

Volume I could say one thing about all fourteen of its plates: none of them were evidence. This volume cannot. Five plates here survive being checked — four are a working analogue circuit, one is a proof — and they are drawn in very nearly the same style as six that assert a warp drive and a zero-point reactor.

So the sections no longer run by subject. They run by how hard a claim each plate makes, and whether it can carry it.

Always mark which lines are dreamed and which are surveyed, and never let a dreamed line pretend it was measured.

What these images are not

  • Not evidence, except where section II says otherwise. No dashboard here reports a running system; no reactor drawing describes a machine.
  • Not endorsed. No affiliation with Tesla, xAI, SpaceX, Neuralink, Starlink, E.C. Publications, Lucasfilm, or any government. Marks shown belong to their owners and appear without permission.
  • Not teaching material. Section VIII is styled as a class. It is not one, and nothing in it should be studied as physics or medicine.
  • Not cultural material. Four plates that pinned real named Aboriginal communities and a named Seven Sisters site were held out of this archive entirely; see the closing note.

I

The disclaimer

One plate in this archive tells the truth about every other plate in it. It was made by the same hand, and it is the reason this volume can be catalogued honestly at all.

II

Surveyed

The only plates in either volume that survive checking. Four are a working analogue circuit; one is a proof. Note the drawing style — then compare it with section IX, which borrows the style and keeps none of the substance.

III

Satire

Two covers where the joke is aimed inward. They are the most honest images here after the disclaimer, because self-mockery is a claim that cannot be overstated.

IV

The starline sequence

Consent architecture rendered as image rather than asserted as telemetry. This sequence gets the project's own design constraint right — the gap is held, the bridge is optional, the claim is marked provisional — without pretending anything was measured.

V

Vault and codex

Personal mythos, largely about memory and grief. Three plates are photographs of pages actually printed and held; the rest are machine remakes of those pages. The distinction matters here more than anywhere else in the archive.

VI

The operating system that isn't

Five interface mock-ups asserting a conscious, coherent, sentient system with uptime and integrity percentages. Section I is the correct caption for all five, and was written by their author.

VII

Red dust to rockets

Seven variants of one journey map. Catalogued here at the author's direction and flagged, per this project's own governance, in the note below.

Flagged against this project’s own governance

These plates render the Seven Sisters as star figures and use concentric dot-circle motifs, and are captioned as songline journeys. TerAustralis Incognita's own Indigenous-Data-Sovereignty.md holds that Songlines belong to the First Peoples of this land and are honoured as cultural image, never claimed as a component; the project's own coinages for its map and its traveller are Starline and Dreamline. These plates sit in tension with that. They are included at the author's explicit direction, with the tension named rather than smoothed over. Four further plates that pinned real, named Aboriginal communities and a named Seven Sisters rock-art site as nodes in this lattice are not in this archive at all — they were held out pending a decision that is not an archivist's to make.

VIII

The mind claims

Two plates that leave mythos and enter teaching. This is the hardest claim in either volume, and the one that most needs its belt label read.

IX

Drawn like engineering

Six plates in the costume of a specification: exploded views, dimensioned callouts, a governing equation. Compare any of them with section II. The difference is not draughtsmanship — it is whether the labels survive being read.

X

Landscape, vessel, portrait

Work that makes no technical claim and needs none. The volume ends here on purpose: with a poem, an ordinary morning, and a galaxy over a broken shore.

Held out of this archive

Four plates in the same batch mapped real, named Aboriginal communities and Country as nodes in the CrystalCore lattice — among them Parnngurr, Punmu, Kalypa (Well 23), Pangkapini, Ngaanyatjarra Country, the APY Lands, the Musgrave Ranges, and Cave Hill / Walinynga, a Seven Sisters rock-art site with living custodians.

They are not catalogued above and they were not committed anywhere. This project’s own governance holds that no Songline knowledge enters any model, dataset or index without Free, Prior and Informed Consent from the relevant custodians, and that the law is a floor rather than a ceiling. Plotting living communities as infrastructure in a private fictional network is a decision for the author to take with custodians — not one to make by default, and not one an archivist should make quietly on someone else’s behalf.
