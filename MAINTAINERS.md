# Stewardship & Governance

This repository operates on a **stewardship model** rather than hierarchical authority. Stewards are recognized experts who guide their domains through consensus-building, design review, and community engagement.

## What is Stewardship?

Stewardship in decentralized systems is:

- **Expertise-based**, not authority-based — stewards earn respect through knowledge and judgment
- **Transparent** — decisions and reasoning are public
- **Revocable** — the community can withdraw recognition if stewardship fails
- **Collaborative** — stewards work together, not in silos
- **Service-oriented** — stewardship is service to the community, not privilege

## Steward Responsibilities

### Design Review
- Read and evaluate new proposals in your domain
- Provide technical feedback on specifications and implementations
- Help shape consensus around design trade-offs
- Ensure alignment with project principles

### Community Engagement
- Respond to questions and discussions in your domain
- Mentor new contributors
- Help people find relevant prior work
- Build and maintain domain expertise across the community

### Quality Assurance
- Verify that implementations match specifications
- Review test coverage and validation approaches
- Identify gaps in documentation
- Flag security or design concerns

### Standard Setting
- Propose improvements to domain standards
- Document conventions and best practices
- Identify deprecated or obsolete approaches
- Recommend when to stabilize or version APIs

## How Stewardship Works

### Proposing Changes

1. **Open an issue** describing the change and its motivation
2. **Seek early feedback** from relevant stewards
3. **Iterate on design** in the issue before implementation
4. **Implement and test** thoroughly
5. **Open a PR** with clear description of changes and trade-offs

### Review Process

- **Technical review** by at least one steward in the relevant domain
- **Specification clarity** — can others implement from this?
- **Test coverage** — are tests and examples included?
- **Interoperability** — does this follow established standards?
- **Security rigor** — have threats been considered?

### Decision Making

When stewards disagree, decisions are made through:

1. **Discussion** — understanding positions and evidence
2. **RFC (Request for Comments)** — structured proposal with alternatives
3. **Consensus building** — seeking acceptable solutions for all parties
4. **Community input** — broader feedback if needed
5. **Documented decision** — reasoning recorded for future reference

### No Merging Without Review

Changes in your domain should only be merged with your (or another steward's) approval. This is a lightweight but clear gate on quality.

## Becoming a Steward

Stewardship is open to anyone who demonstrates:

- **Deep expertise** in a domain
- **Consistent, quality contributions** over time
- **Good judgment** in technical decisions
- **Community respect** and collaborative spirit
- **Clear availability** to engage in review and discussion

To propose yourself or someone else as steward:

1. Open an issue nominating the candidate and domain
2. Describe relevant contributions and expertise
3. The community discusses and reaches consensus
4. Existing stewards ratify the decision

## Removing Stewardship

If a steward is unavailable or no longer engaged:

1. **Take a step back** if you can't commit to review cycles
2. **Hand off** to another expert willing to take the role
3. **Clear communication** helps the community transition smoothly

The community can also propose removing stewardship if someone is consistently unavailable or their judgment is questioned. This would happen through discussion and consensus.

## Communication

- **Community calls** — regular (weekly/biweekly) open discussions of proposals and issues
- **Async-first** — most decisions happen on GitHub in issues and PRs
- **Recorded decisions** — design decisions and rationale documented in the repo
- **Open channels** — stewards stay accessible to the community

---

Stewardship is about service, not status. We value expertise, judgment, and commitment to building excellent decentralized infrastructure together.
