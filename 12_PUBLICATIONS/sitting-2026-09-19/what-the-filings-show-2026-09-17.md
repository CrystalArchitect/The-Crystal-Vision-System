# What the filings actually show about Elon Musk’s control

**By Crystal Elle Arena-Turner**  
Independent strategic analyst · SpaceNews contributor · TerAustralis Incognita  
17 September 2026 (AEST)

People asked what the hard-evidence pack was. Here is the plain answer.

It is not a leak. It is not a conspiracy theory. It is a short reading of documents that are already public: SpaceX’s mid-2026 IPO prospectus, SpaceX’s following quarterly report, Tesla’s September 2025 proxy statement, a June 2026 Form 4, and the published privacy policy for the AI service tied to that corporate stack.

The point of the pack was simple. When commentary gets loud, go back to the text the companies filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

## SpaceX: control is the design

SpaceX’s prospectus sets out a dual-class share structure. Class A shares carry one vote each. Class B shares carry ten. Class B holders, voting as a class, elect 51 percent of the board. Removing Elon Musk from the board and from his roles as chief executive and chairman requires approval from holders of a majority of the Class B voting power.

That is not a slogan. It is charter mechanics.

On voting power, the prospectus itself uses two nearby figures. The cover summary puts Musk at about 82.4 percent of combined voting power after the offering. The beneficial-ownership table shows about 84.4 percent on the basis used there, including certain near-term Class B options. Both numbers belong to the same document. Neither needs invention.

The same reporting season records IPO net proceeds of about $85.675 billion. That is a capital event large enough to reshape an industry. It does not, by itself, dissolve dual-class control.

## Tesla: ownership, pledges, and an option exercise

Tesla’s September 2025 proxy shows Musk beneficially owning 717,323,438 shares, or about 19.8 percent, as of mid-September 2025. It also discloses 235,998,721 shares pledged as collateral for personal indebtedness, and a company policy capping CEO loans collateralised by pledged stock at the lesser of $3.5 billion or 25 percent of the pledged stock’s value. The company stated that, as of 31 December 2024, aggregate loan amounts against pledged value were under 1 percent.

In June 2026, a Form 4 records the exercise of 303,960,630 options at $23.34.

Again: public filing language. Not a secret ledger.

## Why an AI can feel like it “knows” someone

The published privacy policy for the relevant AI service states that it uses public posts shared on X, related engagement data, and internet search data. Anyone who writes in public about Musk, SpaceX, Australian launch politics, or related capital stories is inside that class of text.

That can feel personal. The policy describes a public-data system. It does not, by itself, prove private targeting of any one person.

## What this does not say

This piece does not allege secret crimes. It does not claim Musk personally compiled a private dossier on any Australian analyst. It does not treat emerald-mine folklore, anonymous child-count escalation, or “he owns the government” memes as filing facts.

Separate journalism covers DOGE-era conflicts, government-funding tallies, family reporting, and the January 2026 Epstein-related email releases. Those stories should be read as journalism, with their own sourcing, not smuggled into a prospectus extract.

## Why it mattered to publish the pack

Australia is watching SpaceX capital, allied launch politics, and proposals that treat Australian land as launch geography. When Gina Rinehart’s SpaceX stake and island-offer speech entered the news cycle in June 2026, the public debate needed primary documents as much as opinion.

The hard-evidence pack was built for that: voting control, proceeds, ownership, pledges, and the public-data AI rule — with links to the SEC HTML and receipts for the figures that were re-checked against local copies of those filings.

If you only remember one line, remember this.

**What looks like “hiding” in these records is mostly structural: dual-class control, pledged stock, related companies, and AI trained on the public firehose. The filings force those structures into daylight.**
