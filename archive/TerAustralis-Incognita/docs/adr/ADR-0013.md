# ADR-0013: Extend uniform CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 across the whole portfolio; retire Apache-2.0 and the unlicensed repositories

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Carried out:** 2026-08-08 — eleven days after acceptance; see the
completion note below.

## Context

[`ADR-0010`](ADR-0010.md) settled the licensing question *inside this
repository*: uniform CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, differentiated per-package licensing
not adopted. It did not reach the other repositories, because at the time
the portfolio was not being read as one thing.

The full portfolio review of 2026-07-28 measured what that left behind.
Hashing every tracked non-binary file across all eleven repositories found
**91 distinct file contents published simultaneously under two
incompatible licenses** — CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 in `TerAustralis-Incognita` and
`TerAustralis-Incognita-Code`, Apache-2.0 in `crystalcore`,
`crystal-vision`, and `The-Crystal-Vision`. Not only prose: `clementine.py`,
`crystalcore/expose.py`, `crystalcore/memory.py`, `crystalcore/__main__.py`,
`VISION.md`, `MEMORY.md`, and `SPONSORS.md` are all byte-identical across
the divide.

Both statements come from the same copyright holder, so publishing the
same work under two licenses is entirely permissible. The defect is that
nothing anywhere says it is deliberate. A reader who finds `clementine.py`
in `The-Crystal-Vision` reads Apache-2.0 and concludes they may fork and
modify it; the identical bytes in the umbrella say NoDerivatives and
NonCommercial. Which terms govern is undetermined for every downstream
reader, and the ambiguity regenerates itself every time content is copied
between repositories.

The same conflict also existed *inside* a single repository.
`TerAustralis-Incognita-Code` has a CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 root `LICENSE`, and
36 of its source files carry `SPDX-License-Identifier: CC-BY-NC-ND-4.0`
headers — yet `core/crystal-core/LICENSE` and
`vision/apps/crystal-interface/LICENSE` were Apache-2.0, and both
directories' READMEs said so. Those trees were imported from `crystalcore`
and `crystal-vision` under Migration-Plan Stage 1 and brought their old
sub-licences with them, so a file could assert CC BY-NC-ND in its own
header while the `LICENSE` beside it asserted Apache-2.0.

Separately, six of the eleven repositories carry no licence at all —
`CrystalCore-AERIS`, `CrystalCore.OS`, `crystalcore-os-aeris-vault12`,
`teraustralis-incognita-v2`, `teraustralis-v2-presentation`, and
`CrystalCore.OS-the-Crystal-Architecture-Archive`. Default copyright
applies to those, which is the most restrictive outcome of all and the
one nobody chose. `teraustralis-incognita-v2` additionally declared
`"license": "MIT"` in `package.json` with no MIT text anywhere in the
repository — an unbacked claim.

[`ADR-0009`](ADR-0009.md) asked that anything touching licensing again be
recorded rather than applied silently, having catalogued five accidental
contradictory answers. This is that record.

## Decision

**CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 governs every repository in the portfolio.** The
maintainer chose this directly on 2026-07-28, in preference to splitting
by kind (Apache for code, CC for content) or to documenting the conflict
without resolving it.

Concretely:

- `crystalcore`, `crystal-vision`, and `The-Crystal-Vision` move from
  Apache-2.0 to CC BY-NC-ND 4.0: root `LICENSE` replaced with the same
  text the umbrella carries, `NOTICE` updated, and the licence statements
  in their `README.md` files corrected to match.
- The six unlicensed repositories gain the same `LICENSE` and a `NOTICE`.
- `TerAustralis-Incognita-Code`'s two nested Apache-2.0 sub-licences
  (`core/crystal-core/`, `vision/apps/crystal-interface/`) are replaced
  with the root text, their `NOTICE` files say why a duplicate copy
  exists at all, and their READMEs stop claiming Apache-2.0. The
  repository also gains a root `NOTICE`, which it never had.
- `teraustralis-incognita-v2`'s `package.json` declares
  `"license": "CC-BY-NC-ND-4.0"`, replacing the unbacked MIT claim.
- ADR-0010 is not reopened. This ADR extends its scope from one
  repository to eleven; it does not revisit its reasoning.

## Consequences

- One answer to "what licence is this under" across the whole portfolio,
  which is the thing that has failed four times now (ADR-0006, ADR-0008,
  ADR-0009, ADR-0010 each exist because it failed).
- The 91 dual-published contents stop being ambiguous. They are
  CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 wherever they appear.
- **The Apache-2.0 grant already made cannot be withdrawn.** Apache-2.0
  §2 grants a perpetual, irrevocable copyright licence, so anyone who
  already obtained `crystalcore`, `crystal-vision`, or
  `The-Crystal-Vision` under it keeps those terms for that copy in
  perpetuity, including the right to modify and redistribute it. This
  change binds copies taken from today forward. It is a consequence to
  record, not an obstacle to the decision — but it means the relicensing
  is a narrowing for the future, not a retraction of the past.
- **NoDerivatives applies to patches.** A pull request is a derivative
  work, so outside contribution to any repository in the portfolio now
  requires an explicit grant from the contributor or explicit permission
  from the copyright holder. The portfolio is maintained solo today, so
  this costs nothing now; it is the thing to revisit first if that ever
  changes. Creative Commons themselves recommend against CC licences for
  software for exactly this reason, and that recommendation is noted here
  so a future reader knows it was seen rather than missed.
- Commercial licensing remains the escape hatch, unchanged:
  [`COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md`](../../COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md) and
  [`LICENSING.md`](../../LICENSING.md).
- The duplication that produced the conflict is *not* fixed by this ADR.
  302 tracked files still exist in more than one repository with no
  declared canonical copy, so the same content can still drift apart —
  it just can no longer drift into two different licences. Deduplication
  is its own decision and remains open.
