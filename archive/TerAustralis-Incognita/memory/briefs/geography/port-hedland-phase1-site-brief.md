# Port Hedland — Phase 1 Anchor

Project Brief · TerAustralis Incognita · May 2026

## Purpose

This brief describes Port Hedland, Western Australia, as the proposed first node of the TerAustralis Incognita concept. It is an early-stage outline meant to support discussion and a feasibility conversation — not a development proposal. Anything described here would depend on feasibility studies, regulatory processes and genuine consultation with Traditional Owners.

## Why Port Hedland

Port Hedland is proposed as the anchor site because it brings together a rare combination of existing advantages:

- Established deep-water port — one of the world’s largest bulk-export ports, with heavy infrastructure, shipping channels and logistics capability already in place.
- Proximity to critical minerals — the surrounding Pilbara hosts world-class lithium operations, such as Pilgangoora and Wodgina, alongside other resources.
- Regional energy resources — the broader region has significant natural gas infrastructure relevant to propellant production and processing.
- Indian Ocean access — open ocean to the west, relevant to potential recovery operations.
- Workforce and terrain — an existing industrial workforce and transport links, and remote terrain that could be useful for testing.

## Proposed Phase 1 Functions

As an early-stage concept, a Phase 1 hub at Port Hedland could focus on a small number of clear functions:

- Propellant production and storage, drawing on regional energy resources.
- Recovery and refurbishment infrastructure for reusable launch vehicles.
- Critical-minerals and battery processing — adding value onshore rather than exporting raw material.
- Research activity, including environmental, marine and wildlife studies tied to the site.

## Partnership with Traditional Owners

The Port Hedland area is the Country of the Kariyarra people, who hold recognised native title, and neighbouring Traditional Owner groups also have connections across the wider Pilbara. No part of this concept could proceed without their leadership and consent.

Engagement would begin — before any site selection or technical work — through the proper native title and representative-body channels. It would be built on free, prior and informed consent; benefit-sharing and equity participation; employment and training pathways; independent cultural-heritage protection; and a genuine governance role. This is treated as a firm condition of the concept, not a later approval step.

## Environmental Considerations

Port Hedland sits within a sensitive coastal and marine environment. Any feasibility work would need to address marine ecology, coastal and heritage values, air and water quality, and cumulative impact alongside existing port activity. Environmental assessment and approvals would be early steps, not afterthoughts.

## The Priority Milestone: a Feasibility Study

The single most valuable next step is a serious, independent feasibility study of Port Hedland as a Phase 1 site. A study of this kind would test the core assumptions — energy supply, engineering, environmental constraints, cost and viability — and would either give the concept a credible foundation or show clearly where it needs to change. Either outcome is genuinely useful.

## What Would Be Needed Next

- Early conversations with Traditional Owner representative bodies.
- Input from people with relevant port, energy, environmental and aerospace experience.
- Engagement with Western Australian and Commonwealth agencies through their established processes.
- A defined scope and sponsor for a feasibility study.

This brief is a concept outline. It is intended to open a conversation, and is expected to change as it is tested by people with direct expertise and by the Traditional Owners whose Country this is.
