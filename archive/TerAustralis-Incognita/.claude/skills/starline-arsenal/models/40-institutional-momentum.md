# 40 — Institutional Momentum (The Infrastructure Engines)

**Purpose:** What keeps moving when you stop pushing? What is the organization's inertia?

**CrystalCore mapping:** Register ARCHIVE + LOOM | Primitive sustain() + anchor() | Continuity without constant force

## Core Questions — Run these, do not summarize

1. What structures (processes, roles, incentives, norms) keep work moving without daily direction?
2. What would break or stop if you removed executive attention from it?
3. Where does the organization naturally hold a position, and where does it naturally decay?
4. How do you make a new direction feel like the organization's natural state, not an exception?
5. What is the half-life of a decision—how long does a priority stick before people revert to old patterns?

## Required Concrete Output — No vague labels

- Momentum Audit (what moves on its own | what requires constant push | what decays)
- Sustaining Structure Map (roles, processes, incentives that anchor the work)
- Decay Risk List (what will happen if you stop paying attention, and when)
- Anchor Plan (how to make the new state the default state)

## Evidence → Interpretation → Experiment → Record

- **Evidence:** What happened the last time leadership attention moved away from a priority?
- **Interpretation:** Is the architecture (process, role, incentive) designed to sustain, or only while driving?
- **Experiment:** Stop pushing one priority and measure how long before it decays.
- **Record:** CHRONICLE entry as ARCHIVE (structure state) + LOOM (momentum map)

## Anti-Pattern

Do not confuse a strong leader pushing hard with real momentum. Do not design systems that only work with heroic effort. Do not assume people remember decisions you announced; anchor them in systems, not words.
