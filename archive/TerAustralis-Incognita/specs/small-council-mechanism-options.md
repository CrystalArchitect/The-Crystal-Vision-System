# Small Council #7 — Mechanism Options & Redefinition Path

**Status:** Decision required. This document presents four paths forward after Standards Australia confirmed they do not provide supply-chain verification.

**Context:**  
Roadmap item #7 (Small Council) was designed around an "ASA Statement of Engagement" — a formal verification framework that Standards Australia confirmed (2026-09-04) does not exist in their remit. This document presents real alternatives that DO exist in Australia's regulatory and industry ecosystem, plus a pivot option if verification itself should be redefined.

---

## The Problem Restated

Original Small Council #7 design:
- **Mechanism:** ASA (Standards Australia) "Statement of Engagement" for supply-chain verification
- **Phase 1 Result:** Outreach sent to Lynas, Magellan, ELA, Standards Australia
- **Blocker Found:** Standards Australia's reply (2026-09-03) states supply-chain verification, auditing, endorsement, or accreditation is **not in their remit**
- **Current State:** Phase 2 conversations paused; Magellan withdrawal sent; Lynas/ELA unverified
- **Decision Required:** Pick a real mechanism or redefine what "Small Council" delivers

---

## Option A: Escalate to Australian Space Agency (ASA — Different Body)

**The mechanism:** ASA runs the "Review of Australia's Space Industry Capability" — a formal consultation process with a public submissions list. Unlike Standards Australia, ASA explicitly exists to strengthen Australia's space sector.

**Pros:**
- Real, active mechanism (public submissions list at consult.industry.gov.au)
- Already aligns with roadmap #1 (ASA lodgement pathway)
- Government-endorsed, high credibility
- Explicit focus on "Australian industrial sovereignty" in space

**Cons:**
- Verification is *public comment*, not confidential auditing
- Lynas/Magellan/ELA positioning would be disclosed in a public submission
- Three-step process: submit → ASA reviews → public comment period
- Slower than confidential supply-chain verification

**Timeline:** 4-6 weeks from submission to feedback window opening

**Next action if chosen:** 
- Draft formal submission to ASA on behalf of TerAustralis Incognita
- Name Lynas/Magellan/ELA's roles in sovereign supply chain
- Invite ASA to verify claims during public comment period
- Small Council becomes three companies + ASA, not confidential pre-arrangement

---

## Option B: Industry Standards Bodies (ISO, AERO Compliance)

**The mechanism:** ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 27001 (information security), or aerospace-specific standards (AS/NZS, NADCAP — National Aerospace and Defence Contractors Accreditation Program).

**Pros:**
- Real, widely recognized verification frameworks
- NADCAP explicitly covers aerospace/defense supply chains
- Lynas (rare earths), Liquid Instruments (precision manufacturing), and aerospace suppliers often hold these
- Third-party audited — high credibility

**Cons:**
- Lynas/Magellan/ELA would need to pursue certification themselves (out of our control)
- Small Council becomes "let's check if they're already certified," not "let's jointly verify"
- No novel commitment from participants
- Verification is their private certification record

**Mechanism:** Request existing certification status + audit results (with consent)

**Next action if chosen:**
- Contact Lynas, Magellan, ELA directly to ask current certifications
- Verify via public registries (ISO, NADCAP, etc.)
- Frame Small Council as "assembling the certified supply chain," not driving verification

---

## Option C: Formal Supply Chain Risk Assessment (Financial/Reputational)

**The mechanism:** Third-party supply-chain risk assessor (e.g., Dun & Bradstreet, Bloomberg Supply Chain, or Australian equivalent).

**Pros:**
- Real, commercial service
- Covers financial stability, sanctions risk, reputational risk
- Transparent scoring/methodology
- Lynas/Magellan/ELA already undergo this for investor relations

**Cons:**
- Focuses on *risk* (financial solvency, regulatory), not *sovereignty* (australian-ness)
- Assessment is their private business record
- Small Council is then just "assembling companies that pass risk checks," not unique

**Mechanism:** Request (or purchase) third-party risk assessments; use as verification basis

**Next action if chosen:**
- Identify Australian or Australia-focused supply-chain risk assessor
- Negotiate access to existing assessments on Lynas/Magellan/ELA
- Frame Small Council as "verified low-risk sovereign supply chain"

---

## Option D: Redefine "Small Council" as Capacity Exhibition, Not Verification

**The mechanism:** No external verification — instead, Small Council showcases what the three companies can *actually do together* on a small, real project.

**Concept:**
- Replace verification with demonstration
- Design one real, bounded engineering challenge (e.g., prototype component sourcing end-to-end)
- Lynas → rare earth feedstock
- Liquid Instruments → precision instrumentation
- ELA → integration/launch coordination
- Result: Proof that the supply chain works in practice, not just in theory

**Pros:**
- Honest — doesn't claim verification we can't provide
- Creates real artifact (working prototype component or full sourcing proof-of-concept)
- Addresses the original goal (prove Australian sovereignty is possible) differently
- Circumvents the Standards Australia blocker entirely

**Cons:**
- Requires 3-4 month timeline (Option A-C need 4-6 weeks)
- Significant engineering coordination and cost
- Deliverable is outcome (working hardware), not certification
- Higher risk if any participant can't deliver

**Mechanism:** Co-develop a prototype or micro-project; result is the verification

**Next action if chosen:**
- Identify one realistic engineering challenge (e.g., "source and assemble a critical component for lunar mission architecture")
- Pitch as co-development to Lynas, Magellan, ELA
- Timeline: Phase 2 becomes technical scoping; Phase 3 becomes build; Phase 4 becomes documentation of result
