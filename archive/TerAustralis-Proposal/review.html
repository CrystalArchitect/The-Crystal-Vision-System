01 · Consent and latency — the rules

CrystalCore is Python, in TerAustralis-Incognita-Code. This page does not simulate it. The rules a reviewer is asked to hold:

  1. Label or refuse. Every action carries a belt (science, vision, or docs/gov). Unlabelled speech never reaches the action.
  2. Ask first. A grant request must write a pending record first. If that write fails, the request is refused.
  3. Fail closed. No grant, or a revoke, blocks the next request. Refusal is the default.
  4. Revoke wins in flight. A grant can be taken back. The next action sees the revoke. Light-time delay does not keep a dead grant alive.
  5. Memory is not the model. Facts persist across a process restart. Models are swappable faculties, not the identity.
  6. Delay is labelled. Earth–Mars one-way is typically 4–24 minutes. Any compressed clock on a teaching page was a labelled aid, not a physics claim. The engine does not pretend otherwise.

Identity, if needed, is in the Python engine: two signing halves used as one person (Ed25519 32 bytes + ML-DSA-65 1952 bytes = 1984 bytes). The fingerprint is sixteen hex of the SHA-256 of that blob. A twelve-hex field signature is a different object and is refused. Those numbers are science. They are not a button.

02 · Geographic brief — surveyed facts only

Two pages. No narrative overlay. No site is selected. Belts are labelled on every claim. Full source: GEO-BRIEF-ENGINEER.md.

belt: science

Port and industry already exist

Port Hedland is a working deep-water bulk port on the Pilbara coast. Heavy industry, rail, and a FIFO construction workforce are already there. That is not the same thing as a recovery architecture. Orientation of an existing industrial machine is the claim — not a greenfield city.

belt: science

Western Sydney Aerotropolis is a working precinct

Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) is a 24-hour airport at Badgerys Creek. The operator states passenger services from 25 October 2026. Bradfield City Centre and the Western Sydney Employment Area sit beside it. That is a surveyed precinct. A 150–250 MW compute / robotics load is not contracted and is not forwarded here as surveyed. No site inside the precinct is selected. Invitation, labelled Vision: Sydney Station.

belt: science

Critical minerals, dated

Australia was the world's largest lithium producer in 2024 at roughly 36% of global mine output (Chile ~22%). The Pilbara also hosts iron ore at export scale and rare-earth feedstock (Mt Weld → Lynas). Minerals are a surveyed stock. Propellant and mid-stream processing are not automatic consequences.

belt: science

Legal corridor is in force

The U.S.–Australia Technology Safeguards Agreement was signed 26 October 2023 and entered into force 23 July 2024. It is the treaty-level instrument for U.S. launch vehicles and related technology on Australian soil. AUKUS Pillar 2 sits alongside. The corridor exists. Access to it by an independent Australian entity is not evidenced.

belt: science

Re-entry on Australian soil has been done

Varda's W-2 capsule — launched on a SpaceX rideshare — landed at the Koonibba Test Range, South Australia, on 28 February 2025: the first commercial spacecraft recovered on Australian soil, with further landings since. Approvals, range operations, and recovery logistics have been exercised. That is a range fact, not a Port Hedland berth.

belt: science

Dual-use sensing exists nearby

Space-domain sensing on the north-west coast (including the AUKUS-related radar at Exmouth) supports tracking and re-entry awareness. Earlier drafts placed DARC in the Pilbara; the correction is Exmouth. Sensing is not a crane that can lift a 52-metre stage.

belt: science

Water is a constraint, not a backdrop

The Great Artesian Basin covers ~1.7 million km²; recharge is slow and localised. The Lake Eyre Basin covers ~1.2 million km² and is highly episodic. Any water-dependent pathway (process water, testbed, feedstock) must be engineered against these figures, not around them.

belt: docs-gov

Free, Prior and Informed Consent is a hard door

No site selection, survey, construction, or testbed work proceeds over an identified sacred site or songline path without FPIC from the relevant Traditional Owners, in advance. Floor: Aboriginal Heritage Act 1972 (WA); Native Title Act 1993 (Cth); on the Sydney axis also Aboriginal Land Rights Act 1983 (NSW) and National Parks and Wildlife Act 1974 (NSW). FPIC is not the Native Title right to negotiate. Ceiling: higher than the legal minimum — Juukan Gorge (May 2020) is why. This brief names no specific sacred site.

belt: docs-gov

Appin is named, not selected

Appin, Wollondilly, is Dharawal country (Cataract Gorge). On 17 April 1816 a camp was raided by the 46th Regiment; at least fourteen people were killed. The site was added to the NSW Heritage Register in 2022. On 24 July 1979 fourteen men died in the Appin colliery methane explosion. Aerotropolis West sits in the same south-west basin. That is not one nation claimed for all of Western Sydney, and it is not a site selection. It is why the unlit interior is not treated as empty. The dead are not a hook, and they are not forwarded as a Songline.

belt: docs-gov

What this page does not claim

It does not select a site. It does not assert that Port Hedland is the optimal southern recovery port. It does not claim TerAustralis Incognita can operate a recovery berth. It does not claim a contracted megawatt load at Aerotropolis West. It does not claim a Telstra, Qantas, or Starlink partnership, an aviation install, native satellite voice, or 000 via Direct to Cell. Telstra Satellite Messaging (7 June 2025) and Select Satellite Apps (28 July 2026) are public. Qantas remaining off Starlink Aviation is public. CrystalCore as the layer when even that path is gone is Vision, held in document 08, and is not forwarded here as surveyed.
