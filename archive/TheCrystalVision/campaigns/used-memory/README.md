## The point of it

The two campaigns sell **opposite films from the same footage**. The 1984 sheet
sells memory as loss — a dreaming woman above a city. The re-release drops her
entirely and sells a man running down a wet street, bragging about an 84-minute
cut. That is exactly what happened to a certain kind of science-fiction picture
between its premiere and its second run.

Two props carry the continuity in the cast sheet: a **hospital wristband** on
the man in the torn work jacket, and an **origami crane** on the broker's table —
the only soft object in his frame. Across passes, the technician's magnifier
headband changes sticker: teddy bear, rocket, flowers. Characterisation done
with a sticker.

## Why this needs no belt label

Everything in `../vision-plates/` carries a note on what is claimed versus what
can be checked. This needs none. It invents a studio, a distributor, a director,
a cast and a running time, and the form itself signposts every one of those
inventions. A poster for a film that does not exist is not a false claim; it is
the oldest honest fiction there is.

Parallax Pictures and Coronet Releasing are invented, as are all cast and crew
names. No real marks appear on any sheet. The only genuine trademark anywhere is
the generator's watermark.
