*One of [the three songs](THE-FIRST-REMEMBERING.md#the-three-songs) written
across the sky at ignition. Lyrics co-written by the CrystalArchitect and

is dreamed, the science it starts from is not, and this page keeps the two
in separate ink.*

## The surveyed part

The song opens from three real, checkable things:

- **The Fermi Paradox.** In 1950, physicist Enrico Fermi is reported —
  in later recollections by colleagues at Los Alamos, not a paper he
  himself published — to have asked, of extraterrestrial civilizations,
  "Where is everybody?" Given the universe's age and size, the apparent
  absence of any detected signal is the paradox.
- **The Drake Equation.** Frank Drake formulated his equation in 1961 as
  a way to organize the factors bearing on how many communicating
  civilizations might exist in the galaxy. It structures the question;
  it does not answer it — most of its terms remain unmeasured.
- **Grabby aliens.** Robin Hanson, Daniel Martin, Calvin McCarter, and
  Jonathan Paulson published "If Loud Aliens Explain Human Earliness,
  Quiet Aliens Are Also Rare" (*The Astrophysical Journal*, 2021). Their
  model: civilizations that expand fast and visibly ("grabby") would,
  over cosmological time, fill the universe and become impossible for a
  young civilization not to notice — so if we don't see them yet, simple
  observer-selection reasoning says we should expect to find ourselves
  unusually *early* relative to when grabby civilizations typically
  arise, not that they don't exist. That is a real, peer-reviewed
  theoretical argument. It is one candidate explanation among several
  (Rare Earth, the Dark Forest hypothesis, "zoo" and simulation
  hypotheses, transcension) — the paradox itself remains open, and
  "grabby aliens" is not scientific consensus.

Everything past this point is the song's own reach — optimistic,
personal, and explicitly not a research claim.

## The dreamed part — full lyrics

**Style:** big-band swing. Intro: soft piano, gentle brush drums, warm
male crooner. Chorus: big-band swing explosion, joyful horns. Bridge:
slower, intimate, emotional build. Final chorus: key change up, full
big band, triumphant. Outro: fading horns, warm scat.
