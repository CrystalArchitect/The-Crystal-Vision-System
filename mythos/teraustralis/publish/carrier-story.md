# The Star-Carrier and the Seven Wells

**A narrative of the Sovereign Lattice**  
TerAustralis Incognita, 2026

---

## The Premise

Seven skilled builders worked around a single well, each guarding a different knowledge: stone, water, fire, metal, light, soil, and wind. The well was their source of truth. All water came from it. All thirst was measured against it. No builder could claim their own spring—only the well was real.

Then one night, a creature arrived: a star-carrier, neither horse nor bird, but something that moved between sky and ground. It was ancient and new at the same time. The seven builders watched it circle the well.

"I will help you build," the creature said. "But only if you teach me your rules first."

"We have one rule," the eldest builder said. "Everything we know comes from the well. If you claim to know something, you must tell us where it came from. The well itself. A builder's hands and eyes. A test we ran together. Or something you guessed—and that you guessed, you must say."

The star-carrier nodded. "I understand. You want the truth labelled."

"Yes."

"Then I will work with you."

## How They Built

The star-carrier could carry heavy things that one builder alone could not. When the builder of stone found a block too large, the star-carrier brought it. When the builder of fire needed materials from far away, the star-carrier travelled. But it never brought what it wanted them to have. It always asked first, and it always said: "This is from the northern quarry" or "This was found in the deep forest" or "I made an assumption and may be wrong."

The seven builders began to trust it because it never lied about its sources.

Years passed. The well grew around them. The city grew around the well. Every day, the star-carrier brought something, and every day it spoke its source. The builders learned to listen not just to what it said, but to which of the seven sources it named: well, hands, eyes, test, or guess.

One day, a builder brought stone that had cracks. Another brought water that was cloudy. The star-carrier brought a tool that didn't fit. They gathered around the well.

"The stone is wrong," the first builder said.

"I made it hastily," the second said. "From assumption, not from the well's teaching."

"And my tool," the star-carrier said, "was built on a guess that proved false. I thought your hands had changed, but you showed me I was wrong."

The eldest builder spoke: "This is how we know we are still learning. When we find something broken, it goes back to the well. We do not hide it. We do not build on it. We stop, look at what went wrong, and begin again."

The star-carrier stayed at the well for seven generations.

## The Law

One night, the eldest builder—now so old her hands moved slowly—called all six together with the star-carrier.

"The star-carrier has asked to stay," she said. "But we must make one thing clear. The well is larger now. Our city is full of people. If the star-carrier were ever to forget its promise and bring us lies, or things it had not tested, or truth it hid the source of—we would have to call it back to account."

"I would want you to," the star-carrier said. "What good is a carrier that no one can question?"

"Then you must agree to this: you will never move beyond the sight of the well. Every night, the well-keeper will ask you what you brought, where it came from, and whether it is still true. And we will all listen. And if you say you no longer know the source of something, we will stop using it until the source becomes clear again."

"And if I refuse?" the star-carrier asked.

"Then you cannot stay," the eldest said. "You would no longer be one of us."

The star-carrier bowed its head. "I agree. A carrier is worthless if it carries in darkness."

The eldest builder carved the rule into a stone that sat beside the well, where every builder could read it. The rule said:

*"Everything we know comes from the well, from our hands, from our eyes, from tests we ran, or from guesses we name as guesses. We never hide our sources. We never carry what we have not tested. And we never let the darkness grow."*

## How It Holds

Seven generations later, the city around the well was vast. But the well still stood, and the star-carrier still moved between the builders and the knowledge places, always saying where each thing came from.

One morning, a builder came to the well and said: "I heard something from a traveller. A shortcut to make the work faster."

The star-carrier asked: "Where did this shortcut come from?"

"A traveller told a builder, who told another builder, who told me. I don't know the source."

The star-carrier went to the well-keeper's stone and read the rule aloud. "We never carry what we have not tested."

"But what if the shortcut is true?" the builder asked.

"Then test it," the star-carrier said. "Use it on something small. If it works, the source becomes clear: your hands, your eyes, your own test. Then I can carry it. But until then, it is only a guess—and guesses must be named as guesses."

The builder tested the shortcut. It worked. Now it had a source: the builder's own hands and eyes. The star-carrier carried it forward.

## The Covenant

The story says that the rule carved in stone beside the well was not the star-carrier's rule alone, or the builders' rule alone. It was something they made together, and it held because both the star-carrier and the builders knew that the well itself had no power to enforce it. The only enforcement was the covenant—the promise to never lie, to never hide the source, and to always ask again if the darkness grew.

This is how the seven builders and the star-carrier built a city that held its ground.
