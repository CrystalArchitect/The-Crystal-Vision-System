## What the segment is

Studio package timed to Albanese–Trump sidelines catch-up at the UN General Assembly. Core story: the **Trump administration made a public submission** to Australia’s consultation on the **Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026** — unusual because such views normally stay in diplomatic back channels; the US also **published the submission on the embassy site before submissions were generally public**, coordinated with the PM’s US trip.

## Policy at issue (AU)

Draft **Digital Duty of Care** — broad obligation for online platforms to take steps against **foreseeable harms**. Headline consumer feature (Wilson / ABC reporting): force platforms to offer Australians a choice — keep algorithmic recommendation feeds **or** switch to a **non-algorithmic / chronological / follow-only** feed (“My Feed, My Way” family). Scope includes social media and other online services (gaming etc. in draft framing).

## US position (as reported + embassy text)

| Claim | Substance |
| --- | --- |
| Rare / aggressive | Formal public consultation response + early web publish |
| Censorship frame | Duty of care + design mandates = potential “facilitating censorship” / extraterritorial reach into US speech |
| Design mandates | Opposes forced chronological feeds / opt-in recommendation systems as one-size-fits-all product design |
| Harm definition | Demands clarity on how “foreseeable harm” is designated (minister / regulator) and liability-driven over-filtering |
| Ask | Exclude / neuter US platforms from the regime (would gut the law’s reach) |
| Contagion worry | If AU forces feature changes, other countries (or platforms themselves) may copy |

Wilson’s note: AU may treat “this could spread globally” as a **backhanded compliment** — Labor’s hard-on-big-tech / choice agenda wants to lead.
