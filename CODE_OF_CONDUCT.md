# Community Guidelines

We're building decentralized, sovereign infrastructure. That requires high standards of technical rigor, intellectual honesty, and collaborative problem-solving.

## Our Values

### Technical Rigor
- Proposals should be backed by reasoning, evidence, or prior work
- Trade-offs should be explicit and discussed
- Test vectors and verification examples matter
- Specifications should be precise enough for independent implementation

### Intellectual Honesty
- Admit uncertainty and limitations
- Change your mind when evidence warrants it
- Acknowledge prior work and influences
- Distinguish between what you know and what you believe

### Collaborative Problem-Solving
- Disagreement about ideas, evidence, and design trade-offs is healthy
- Listen to understand, not just to reply
- Seek common ground while respecting legitimate differences
- Build on each other's insights

### Openness
- Share knowledge and reasoning openly
- Help people find their way in the project
- Mentor contributors at all levels
- Assume good faith unless proven otherwise

## Expected Behavior

### In Issues & Pull Requests
- Comment on code, design, and ideas — not people
- Provide reasoning when you disagree
- Ask clarifying questions before objecting
- Acknowledge good contributions from others
- Respond to feedback in good faith

### In Discussions & Meetings
- Listen to understand differing perspectives
- Speak to the issue, not the person
- Help bring people in, not shut them down
- Follow up on commitments you make
- Respect others' time and availability

### In Design Decisions
- Propose alternatives, not just objections
- Consider evidence and prior work
- Explain your reasoning clearly
- Be willing to compromise on implementation while staying true to principles
- Document decisions for future reference

## What We Don't Tolerate

### Bad-Faith Arguments
- Arguments designed to derail discussion rather than advance it
- Repeated objections to settled decisions without new evidence
- Attacks on contributors' competence or motives (not their ideas)
- Demands for special consideration that others aren't given

### Deliberate Misinformation
- Presenting false claims as fact
- Suppressing relevant information or prior work
- Misleading others about your credentials or involvement
- Lying about context or motivation

### Harassment & Abuse
- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissive language
- Unwanted contact outside project channels
- Coordinated attacks or targeting
- Threats or intimidation

## Resolving Issues

### Informal Resolution
Most issues resolve through direct conversation:

1. Point out the concern clearly and respectfully
2. Assume good intent and give people a chance to respond
3. Focus on the behavior, not the person
4. Move forward once there's understanding

### Escalation
If informal resolution doesn't work:

1. Ask for a conversation with the stewards in your domain
2. Describe the issue, the attempts to resolve it, and what you're asking for
3. Stewards will listen to all sides and propose resolution
4. If you disagree with the outcome, there's a final review option

### Enforcement
For severe or repeated violations:

- The community and stewards can ask someone to step back from contributing
- Serious cases (harassment, abuse) may result in permanent removal
- Decisions will be documented and explained

## Reporting Concerns

If you see behavior that concerns you:

1. **If safe**, talk directly with the person involved
2. **If not comfortable**, reach out to a steward privately
3. **For serious issues**, contact the project maintainers directly
4. **All reports** are treated seriously and handled confidentially

## Scope

These guidelines apply to:

- All project repositories and discussions
- Pull requests, issues, and code reviews
- Synchronous meetings and calls
- Public project communications

They don't govern your personal conduct outside the project, but they do apply to how you represent the project publicly.

## Our Commitment

- We're committed to making this a welcoming community for people of all backgrounds
- We actively work against bias and discrimination
- We correct our own mistakes and learn from feedback
- We hold ourselves and each other to these standards

---

Decentralized infrastructure depends on trust, transparency, and good-faith collaboration. These guidelines exist to protect that foundation. Thank you for being part of building something better.
