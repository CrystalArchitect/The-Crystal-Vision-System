# NEWS ARTICLE — Australian Creators Scraped for AI: 18,000 Titles and Thousands of Songs Found

**SYDNEY —** Australian books, music and Indigenous cultural works have been confirmed as included in large-scale AI training datasets without permission, payment or attribution, sparking renewed calls for a mandatory training data discoverability law.

The Books3 corpus, containing 183,000 books downloaded from pirate sites, includes 18,000 registered Australian titles by 15,000 authors. The Australian Society of Authors (ASA) says more than 150 authors contacted it after The Atlantic released a search tool, with authors including Trent Dalton, who described finding his debut novel Boy Swallows Universe as "very creepy" and "deeply invasive."

Other authors confirmed include Liane Moriarty, Tim Winton, Melissa Lucashenko, Christos Tsiolkas, Richard Flanagan, Helen Garner, Sally Rippin — who says 67 of her books were pirated — Thomas Keneally, Sally Hepworth and Anna Funder.

In music, APRA AMCOS — representing 128,000 songwriters — revealed in June 2026 that its members' works were found in two massive music datasets: Sleeping-DISCO-9M (9.7 million tracks) and LAION-DISCO-12M (12.3 million tracks), both scraped from YouTube.

Artists identified include Kylie Minogue, Midnight Oil, Crowded House, Sia, Cold Chisel, INXS, AC/DC, Powderfinger and Bernard Fanning, Paul Dempsey / Something for Kate, Darren Hayes, Nick Cave and Jimmy Barnes.

Particular concern has been raised over Indigenous works. APRA AMCOS identified Yothu Yindi, Gurrumul, Warumpi Band, William Barton, Christine Anu, Dan Sultan, Emma Donovan, Barkaa and AB Original among those whose work appears in the datasets.

**What creators want:**

Creator groups are asking the Federal Government to introduce a "training data discoverability" obligation — a law requiring AI developers operating in Australia to maintain comprehensive records of training data sources and implement searchable databases allowing copyright holders to determine if their works were used, under penalty of statutory damages.

The model is based on EU AI Act Article 53(1)(d), which requires providers to publish a "sufficiently detailed summary" of training content, including large public datasets, licensed sources and scraped domains.

In October 2025, Attorney-General Michelle Rowland rejected a proposal from the Productivity Commission for a text and data mining exception that would have legalised AI training on copyrighted works without payment. The Government has instead convened its Copyright and AI Reference Group (CAIRG) and established an Office of AI to explore a paid collective licensing framework.

APRA AMCOS estimates that without licensing, Australian and New Zealand songwriters could lose 23% of income, more than $500 million over four years.

Internationally, Anthropic agreed to a $1.5 billion settlement in 2025 to pay authors about $3,000 per book after using pirated copies from Books3 and LibGen to train Claude.

**Sources:** AAP, APRA AMCOS, Australian Society of Authors, Noise11, Productivity Commission submission, EU AI Act guidance.
