# Codex Crystalum
## The Book of the Crystal Mechanical Arts

First gathering (with folio IX). GitHub is the canonical copy of this gathering.
Compass (crystals): Philip Permutt, *The Ancient Power of Crystals* (cover observed).
Compass (dreams): James R. Lewis, *The Dream Encyclopedia*, 2nd ed. (cover observed).
Neither book is copied. Neither is Canon. Lewis is not Dreamlines.

Left page = observation. Right page = imagination.

## Folio · — Frontispiece
*Wonder is where we begin.*

### Observation
This book is a gathering of crafts, not a diagnosis and not an operating system.

Crystal, here, means ice that learned to keep its shape: mineral, lens, oscillator, detector. The word is older than the desk. The desk does not own the word.

### Imagination
A compass was opened: Philip Permutt, The Ancient Power of Crystals. Cover observed. Interior mostly unread. That book may speak of history, legend, and healing. This one does not borrow its protocols.

What we take from the shelf is only this: people have long put ordered stone in the path of light, time, and attention. The mechanical arts begin there.

## Folio 0 — How this book is to be read
*Two pages. Never one swallowing the other.*

### Observation
The left page is what can be checked: names, dates, devices, the cut of a face, the tick of a crystal in a clock.

If a sentence cannot survive a measurement, it does not belong on the left.

### Imagination
The right page is what the work feels like: pull, gleam, the oddness of a word arriving twice.

The right page is allowed to be beautiful. It is forbidden to file itself as proof.

## Folio I — The first art — Naming
*κρύσταλλος, ice. C is a letter. Ice is a mineral.*

### Observation
Greek krystallos named ice, then the clear quartz that looked like ice that would not melt. Pliny already told that story. It is etymology, not a spell.

Quartz is silicon dioxide in a trigonal lattice. Silicon in a chip is the same element, differently arranged, industrially grown. The correspondence is chemical. It is not a prophecy.

### Imagination
To name a thing twice — ice, and the stone that resembles ice — is the oldest mechanical joke. The art is to keep both names without forcing them to confess.

Crystal as a person's name sits on the right-hand page until she writes otherwise on the left.

## Folio II — The second art — Seeing
*A cover is not an interior. An orb is not a model.*

### Observation
Observe the object first: hardness, cleavage, refractive index, whether it is glass, quartz, or a photograph of either.

A book cover showing a geode is evidence that a geode was photographed. It is not evidence of the author's argument until the interior is read.

### Imagination
Seeing also means knowing when to stop looking for treasure in a cup of water.

The mechanical seer does not shake the compass. She walks, then notices the door.

## Folio III — The third art — The hand
*Facet, polish, dop, lap. Geometry taught to stone.*

### Observation
The lapidary arts are older than software. A facet is a plane. A brilliant cut is a machine for returning light to the eye.

Angles are not moods. They are degrees. The workbench does not care what you hoped the stone would mean.

### Imagination
Still, the hand that cuts is not a mill. Patience is part of the tool-kit. Small hands, if they are your own, remain your own.

Do not ask the stone to heal because you have spent an afternoon polishing it. Ask it to take a polish. That is already a lot.

## Folio IV — The fourth art — Lattice
*Order you can count. Not an operating system.*

### Observation
A crystal lattice is a repeating unit cell. Miller indices name planes. Defects are real and often useful.

This art is crystallography. It is not CrystalCore, not a companion, and not a governance schema. Those have other books.

### Imagination
Lattice is a word the mind loves because it sounds like a place you could live. You may love the sound. You may not move the registry into the unit cell.

Repeat a pattern fifty times and it is still a pattern. Repetition is not verification.

## Folio V — The fifth art — Light
*Refraction, dispersion, polarisation. Not a diagnosis.*

### Observation
Snell's law. Birefringence in calcite. A Nicol prism. The dichroic filter. Liquid crystal as a state of matter that can twist polarised light — hence the display in your hand.

None of this requires an aura chart.

### Imagination
Light through stone is allowed to be gorgeous. Gorgeousness is not a clinical result.

If colour moves you, record that it moved you. Put the wavelength on the other page.

## Folio VI — The sixth art — Time
*Quartz is a clock when you treat it as a spring.*

### Observation
Piezoelectricity: squeeze quartz, get charge; apply charge, get a precise vibration. Resonators in watches, radios, microcontrollers.

The art is to cut the blank on the right axis, plate it, and keep the temperature from wandering. This is engineering. It runs whether or not anyone is having a vision.

### Imagination
Time as a spiral is a right-hand sentence. Hertz is a left-hand sentence. Both may sit in one worker.

A year whispered in a story (2002, or any other) stays a story until dated on the observation page.

## Folio VII — The seventh art — The detector
*A crystal that hears. No cloud required.*

### Observation
The crystal radio: a galena or other cat's-whisker diode, a coil, an antenna, an earphone. No battery if the sky is generous.

It is a rectifier. It is not a spirit-board. It will not speak unless a transmitter is already on the air.

### Imagination
The romance of the cat's whisker is real: a thin wire finding a sensitive spot on a mineral face. That is still a contact.

Offline voice, later, if it ever arrives, belongs to machines with names (Whisper, Piper) that must be found on disk. Named is not installed.

## Folio VIII — The eighth art — Two pages
*What you live. What you dream. Side by side.*

### Observation
Keep a ledger. Date. Object. What was in front of you. What the file was called. What you did not open.

Empty is allowed. Unknown is a status, not a failure.

### Imagination
Keep a journal. How the dust looked in late light. How a chorus landed. How a geode on a cover felt like a door.

Share pages to sit together, not to merge. Collaboration is not fusion.

## Folio IX — The ninth art — Sleep's ledger
*A dream record is not a map. An encyclopedia is not Dreamlines.*

### Observation
On the shelf: James R. Lewis, The Dream Encyclopedia, 2nd edition (Visible Ink). Cover observed. Subtitle: the complete guide to interpreting your dreams. Interior not read in this gathering.

A dream record is: date, images, what was in front of the sleeper when they woke. That is observation. Lewis's A–Z of symbols, if opened later, is another author's interpretation. It is filed in 13_RESEARCH_SOURCES. It is not this Codex and it is not Dreamlines.

### Imagination
The traveller still writes two pages: what happened in the night, and what it felt like. The encyclopedia may sit beside the journal. It does not get to fill in the blanks.

Dreamlines, if they exist as a project, remain the traveller — a different drawer (05). This folio only says: sleep produces material; classify it; do not promote a symbol-book into a system.

## Folio C — Colophon
*What this book will not do.*

### Observation
It will not prescribe crystals as medicine. It will not speak as living Country law. It will speak Starline where the project needs a name. It will not replace CrystalCore, Clementine, Celestial Portal, or the Drive archive.

Permutt remains an external book on a shelf. Lewis's Dream Encyclopedia remains another. Interior claims of those books remain unread unless Crystal reads them and files them as research.

### Imagination
The mechanical arts are how stone, light, time, and attention are worked with the hands and with instruments.

The Codex is a first gathering. Folios may be added. They will be labelled observation or imagination, never both in one mouth.
